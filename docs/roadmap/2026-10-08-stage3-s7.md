# Stage 3 of 2.0: S7 built and accepted

S7 is the seventh step of stage 3 ([stage3.md](../v2/stage3.md)): the tool path runs on
the fixture
([stage3-steps-adapters.md](../v2/stage3-steps-adapters.md#s7-the-tool-path-on-the-fixture-codex),
[stage3-fixture.md](../v2/stage3-fixture.md#the-tool-transport)). It was built in six
commits, `89d5d72`..`0a8aac9`, and accepted on October 8, 2026 after two review rounds on
the Codex side, with six fix commits, `1bb8c4e`..`ac22859`, landed as new commits on top.

## The commits

- `89d5d72` — **one neutral input for the endpoint**: a turn started and ended, a call
  seen, a call's result, a tool startup failed. The Codex event parser and the Claude Code
  hook path became its callers; the ticket tests run through it on a transport of no
  harness.
- `cff79a8` — **the turn-id contract**: a transport declares in its binding that its turn
  ids are never reused; a later attempt in the same turn marks only when the ticket and
  the record both carry the declaration, on the same transport.
- `63e3512` — **the turn's latest start in the core**: the neutral input's timed start is
  recorded in the mailbox, and pending and the turn end take the later of that record and
  the telemetry file.
- `0c3fdf7` — **the fixture's tool transport**: descriptors built from the command table,
  the endpoint's transport role checked per request on the exact process, the call run in a
  child of the wrapper's image.
- `bee2d2e` — **the neutral rig** in `test/toolrig`: every `bridge/server` oracle rebuilt
  on the fixture's own program, the gaps E4, T1, T2, T4, T5, T6, T7 and T11 closed, and the
  endpoint finishing its transport calls as it closes.
- `0a8aac9` — **one workflow scenario per tool** of the set, through the fixture program:
  inbox, send, pending, whoami, retry and list.

## The first review round

The first three commits passed; the last three needed changes.

- **R1.** The transport was checked at its hello only, so a request sent after the
  transport was withdrawn still ran.
- **R2.** `Close` cut calls it had already taken before their children confirmed: it closed
  the listener and the children's connections at once.
- **R3.** `tool-retry` passed when every retry was refused: the first send supplied the
  heads-up, and a refusal mentioning the receipt satisfied the rest. The tool scenarios had
  no controls.
- **R4.** The mutant M3, the acknowledgment ignoring an end on record, survived both rigs.
  It is an independent T7 check — an end another process recorded, which the gate never
  heard of — and a probe killed it.
- **R5.** The workflow fixture sent its turn starts without a time, although its program
  controls its own start.
- **The acknowledgment racing the end.** Every fixture inbox call waited up to ten seconds
  for its read to commit, hidden and unchecked; and the complementary order — an end
  captured before the acknowledgment's gate check — had no deterministic test.

## The fixes

- `1bb8c4e` — the transport is checked again as its request arrives, before the command's
  check; `Close` refuses new calls but keeps the socket open to the endpoint's own
  children until the last call it took has answered.
- `19aaa2d` — two neutral-rig tests: an end on record the gate never heard refuses the
  acknowledgment (M3 kills it alone), and an end captured before the acknowledgment's check
  leaves the letter unread, out of that end's report, claimed once by a later read (the
  gate mutant kills it alone).
- `6d3446d` — the fixture program times its turn starts, and `fixture-turn-start` reads
  two increasing starts in the mailbox; the ten-second wait became `awaitRead`, the inbox
  scenario's own choice, with its success recorded and required; `tool-retry` judges the
  retry's parsed answer; each tool scenario has a product control, and the controls run
  crosswise ([testing-tools.md](../testing-tools.md)).
- `14aa9e1` — the tool cases moved from `testing-cases.md`, past 400 lines, to
  `testing-tools.md`.
- `396e862` — a third `Close` window: a child held after its hello was admitted and before
  it asks; a mutant that stops draining that connection survived the first two windows.
- `ac22859` — the declaration on only one end, record or ticket, proves no turn; one of
  its two mutants was caught by no earlier test.

The second round accepted all twelve commits with no further edits.

## The time the checks took

The first round ran the five checks on a clean detached worktree of each of the six
commits, one commit at a time at `-p 2`: 35 to 49 minutes each, four hours and nineteen
minutes in all, nearly all of it the three `-race` test runs of 11 to 17 minutes each,
with `internal/inbox` and `internal/channel` the slowest packages. The suite and the
crosswise check took eleven minutes together.

The owner decided on October 8, 2026 that the five checks are the condition for the
commit that lands a step, its last; intermediate commits only build (`go build`,
`go vet`); fixes after review run the affected packages while the work goes on and the
five checks once before acceptance; no clean worktree per commit; `-p 4` when no other
heavy run is going and `-p 2` when one is. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#checks) states the
rule. The fix round ran them once, on `ac22859` at `-p 4`: 28 minutes, the three test runs
585, 545 and 534 seconds; the suite took six and a half minutes, the crosswise check two
and a half.
